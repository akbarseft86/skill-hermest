# Influence & Persuasion Psychology

The 7 principles of persuasion applied to marketing, copywriting, sales, and product design. Based on Robert Cialdini's "Influence" and "Pre-Suasion" — the most cited persuasion research in history.

## The 7 Principles of Influence

### 1. RECIPROCITY
**Principle**: People feel obligated to give back when they receive something first.

**Marketing Applications:**
- Lead magnets (free guide, template, tool)
- Free trials / freemium models
- Unexpected bonuses after purchase
- Free valuable content before asking for sale
- Personalized recommendations or audits

**Copywriting Triggers:**
- "Here's a free [resource] — no strings attached"
- "We created this guide specifically for [audience]"
- "Before we tell you about our product, here's something useful..."

**Key**: Give FIRST, give something VALUABLE, give something PERSONALIZED

### 2. COMMITMENT & CONSISTENCY
**Principle**: People want to be consistent with their past actions and stated beliefs.

**Marketing Applications:**
- Micro-commitments: Small yeses that lead to big yeses
- Quizzes/assessments (they commit to their answers)
- Email opt-in → free resource → paid product (escalation)
- "Are you the type of person who...?" identity questions
- Public commitments (sharing goals, joining challenges)

**Copywriting Triggers:**
- "If you're someone who [positive trait], then..."
- "You've already taken the first step by [action]"
- "Based on what you told us, here's what we recommend..."
- Footer: "Join X others who committed to [goal]"

**Funnel Design:**
1. Micro-commitment (click, quiz, free download)
2. Small commitment (email, follow, share)
3. Medium commitment (webinar, trial, small purchase)
4. Large commitment (main offer)

### 3. SOCIAL PROOF
**Principle**: People look to others to determine correct behavior, especially in uncertainty.

**Marketing Applications:**
- Testimonials (specific results > vague praise)
- Case studies with numbers
- User counts: "Join 50,000+ marketers"
- Real-time activity: "12 people bought this today"
- Expert endorsements
- Media logos: "As featured in..."
- UGC (user-generated content)

**Social Proof Hierarchy (strongest → weakest):**
1. Celebrity/influencer endorsement
2. Expert authority endorsement
3. User testimonials with specific results
4. Large user numbers
5. Peer recommendations
6. Certification/awards

**Copywriting Triggers:**
- "[X] people can't be wrong"
- "The #1 choice for [audience]"
- "[Name], [Title] says: '[quote]'"
- "97% of customers report [result]"

### 4. AUTHORITY
**Principle**: People follow the lead of credible, knowledgeable experts.

**Marketing Applications:**
- Display credentials, certifications, experience
- Cite research and data in your content
- Get featured in recognized media
- Author books or publish research
- Use professional design (authority cue)
- Partner with recognized brands

**Authority Signals:**
- Titles and credentials
- Uniform/professional appearance
- Published works
- Media appearances
- Awards and recognition
- Association with established institutions

### 5. LIKING
**Principle**: People say yes to those they like. Liking comes from similarity, compliments, cooperation, and familiarity.

**Marketing Applications:**
- Brand personality that matches audience
- Storytelling (personal, relatable stories)
- Show the human behind the brand
- Use language/slang your audience uses
- Compliment your audience genuinely
- Shared enemies (common frustrations)

**Copywriting Triggers:**
- "We're [audience] too, and we get it"
- "You're already ahead of 90% of people because..."
- "Like you, we're frustrated with [shared enemy]"
- Behind-the-scenes content showing real people

### 6. SCARCITY
**Principle**: People want more of what they can have less of. Loss aversion is 2x stronger than gain motivation.

**Marketing Applications:**
- Limited time offers (real deadlines)
- Limited quantity ("Only 12 spots left")
- Exclusive access (waitlist, invite-only)
- Early bird pricing
- Disappearing bonuses
- Seasonal/event-based offers

**Types of Scarcity:**
- **Quantity**: Limited spots, limited stock
- **Time**: Deadline, countdown timer
- **Access**: Invitation only, application required
- **Information**: "Only members can access..."

⚠️ **WARNING**: Fake scarcity destroys trust. Only use REAL scarcity.

### 7. UNITY
**Principle**: People say yes to those they consider "one of us" — shared identity and belonging.

**Marketing Applications:**
- Tribe/community building
- "We" language (inclusive)
- Shared identity markers
- In-group rituals and language
- Membership/belonging signals
- Co-creation with customers

**Copywriting Triggers:**
- "For [specific tribe] only"
- "Welcome to the [community name]"
- "You're one of us"
- Using insider language your tribe recognizes

## Pre-Suasion: What Happens BEFORE the Message

**Key insight**: What people pay attention to RIGHT BEFORE your message determines how they respond to it.

**Pre-Suasion Techniques:**
1. **Priming**: Expose them to related concepts first
2. **Anchoring**: Set a reference point before revealing your number
3. **Framing**: How you present the question shapes the answer
4. **Attention channeling**: Direct focus to favorable attributes

**Application to Ads & Landing Pages:**
- Before showing price → Show the value/ROI first (anchoring)
- Before asking for commitment → Get small agreement first (consistency)
- Before presenting your solution → Agitate the problem (framing)
- Before CTA → Show testimonials of people like them (social proof + unity)
