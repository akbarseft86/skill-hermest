# Jobs to Be Done (JTBD)

Framework for discovering innovation based on a fundamental truth: customers don't buy products — they "hire" them to do a specific job in their lives.

Based on Clayton Christensen's "Competing Against Luck" (Harvard Business School).

## Core Concept

**Job to Be Done** = the progress a customer wants to make in specific circumstances.

Every job has three inseparable dimensions:
1. **Functional**: The practical task to accomplish
2. **Emotional**: How they want to feel (or avoid feeling)
3. **Social**: How they want to be perceived by others

## Job Statement Format

"When I [SITUATION/CONTEXT], I want to [MOTIVATION/PROGRESS], so I can [DESIRED OUTCOME]."

Examples:
- "When I'm commuting alone, I want to make the time productive, so I can feel like I'm growing professionally."
- "When I'm launching a new product, I want to understand my customers' real motivations, so I can create messaging that resonates."

## The Four Forces of Progress

When a customer considers switching (from current → new solution):

**Forces pushing FOR change:**
1. 🔥 **Push of Current Situation**: Frustration with status quo
2. 🧲 **Pull of New Solution**: Attraction to new possibility

**Forces pushing AGAINST change:**
3. 😨 **Anxiety of New Solution**: Fear/uncertainty about switching
4. 🛋️ **Habit of Current Behavior**: Comfort with what they know

**To get someone to switch**: Forces FOR change must exceed forces AGAINST.

**Application to marketing:**
- Increase Push: Agitate the pain of staying
- Increase Pull: Paint vivid picture of new reality
- Decrease Anxiety: Guarantees, testimonials, free trial
- Decrease Habit: Make switching frictionless, migration tools

## Big Hire vs. Little Hire

- **Big Hire**: The moment of purchase decision
- **Little Hire**: The moment of actual use/engagement

Both must be won. Winning the Big Hire but failing the Little Hire = churn.

**Churn diagnosis questions:**
- Are users completing onboarding? (Little Hire friction)
- Do they return after first use? (Little Hire value delivery)
- At what point do they stop engaging? (Little Hire failure point)

## Customer Interview Framework

### Timeline Interview (Past Purchase)

Map the journey from "first thought" to "purchase and beyond":

1. **First Thought**: When did you first start looking for a solution?
2. **Passive Looking**: What did you notice but not act on?
3. **Active Looking**: When did you start seriously searching?
4. **Decision**: What tipped you to choose THIS solution?
5. **Consumption**: What was first use like?
6. **Satisfaction**: Did it do the job you hired it for?

### Key Interview Questions

**Situational:**
- "Tell me about the last time you [relevant behavior]..."
- "Walk me through what happened right before you decided to..."
- "What was going on in your life when you started looking?"

**Motivation:**
- "What were you hoping would change?"
- "What would 'success' have looked like?"
- "What did you try before this?"

**Emotional:**
- "How did it make you feel when [problem occurred]?"
- "What were you worried about when switching?"
- "How did you feel after using it for the first time?"

## Non-Obvious Competition

Your real competitors aren't who you think. Products compete at the JOB level:

| The Job | Obvious Competitors | Non-Obvious Competitors |
|---------|-------------------|----------------------|
| "Help me relax after work" | Netflix, Spotify | Wine, Bath bombs, Meditation app |
| "Make me feel productive" | Todoist, Notion | Bullet journal, Morning routine, Coffee shop |
| "Help me feel connected" | WhatsApp, Instagram | Book clubs, Gym classes, Co-working spaces |

**To find your real competition, ask:** "What did you use/do BEFORE you found our product?"

## Applying JTBD to Marketing

1. **Messaging**: Lead with the JOB, not features
   - ❌ "Our app has 50+ templates"
   - ✅ "Create professional proposals in 10 minutes"

2. **Positioning**: Position against the JOB, not competitors
   - ❌ "Better than Competitor X"
   - ✅ "For [situation], get [progress] without [pain]"

3. **Segmentation**: Segment by JOB, not demographics
   - ❌ "Women 25-35 in urban areas"
   - ✅ "Busy professionals who want to eat healthy without meal prep"

4. **Features**: Build features that serve the job
   - Every feature should answer: "Which job does this help complete?"

## Example Prompts

- "Help me write interview questions to discover the job our customers hire our app for"
- "Our signup-to-active rate is 20%. Diagnose why users aren't completing the Little Hire"
- "What jobs might compete with our [product] that aren't obvious competitors?"
- "Write a job statement for someone buying a [product/service]"
- "Map the Four Forces for someone switching from [current solution] to our product"
